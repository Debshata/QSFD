The project followed an iterative research-and-validation approach organised in twelve one-week planning cycles between 20 July 2026 and 11 October 2026. Each cycle had the same loop: choose the next experiment or component, implement it, validate it with tests or statistics, record the result in the repository, and revise the plan. Work was divided by the three roles in Table~\ref{tab:roles}. Experimental protocols for the Layer~1 to Layer~2 integration were written and committed \emph{before} the experiment ran, to prevent post-hoc selection. The schedule, owners and planning points in this chapter are a drafted baseline built from the team's twelve weeks and aligned with the dates recorded in the repository; the points are relative planning estimates, not measured effort, and the team may replace them with its own records.

\section{Project Timeline}
The twelve weeks are listed in Table~\ref{tab:weekly}. The project was split into twenty-five work packages (Table~\ref{tab:wbs}), shared so that each member carries the same planned effort of 40 of the 120 planning points (Fig.~\ref{fig:pm-share}), with six milestones (Table~\ref{tab:milestones}). Four milestones are anchored to repository evidence: the first commit on 25 August 2026 (week~6, which also holds the first \path{ibm_fez} QML hardware run), the decisive Layer~1 validation on 25 September (week~10), the Layer~2 core, integration experiment and notebooks on 30 September (week~11), and the pqcrypto comparison, replicated IBM hardware campaign and report package on 7 October (week~12). Weeks~1--5 (data, classical baselines, classical cryptography, literature) precede the first commit; their allocation is a planning estimate.

Fig.~\ref{fig:pm-gantt} shows the Gantt chart. Fig.~\ref{fig:pm-burndown} shows the burndown of the 120 planning points: the blue line is the remaining work implied by the work-package schedule and the dashed line is an ideal linear burndown. The drafted schedule stays above the ideal line until the final week (the gap is widest in weeks~7--8, at roughly 25 points) because the larger quantum and cryptography work packages start late; about 67 of the 120 points fall in the last four weeks, when the decisive Layer~1 validation, the Layer~2 work, the hardware campaign and the report were concentrated. This back-loaded profile is a schedule risk and is recorded as such in the risk assessment. Fig.~\ref{fig:pm-workload} shows the planned weekly workload per role and Fig.~\ref{fig:pm-burnup} the cumulative planned progress.
