\chapter*{Abstract}
The Quantum-Secure Fraud Detection System (QSFD) is a research prototype that studies fraud detection and transaction security for digital banking in the era of quantum computing. Layer~1 compares classical and quantum machine-learning fraud models; a 20-seed paired experiment shows that a local-sum measurement observable significantly improves a matched quantum neural network (mean PR-AUC difference \LsDelta{}, bootstrap 95\% CI [\LsCIlo{}, \LsCIhi{}]), while a matched classical control and XGBoost show no verified incremental quantum value. Layer~2 implements AES-256-GCM, RSA, ECC, ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA and compares a pure-Python reference with the compiled \texttt{pqcrypto} backend (speedups \SpeedupMin{}--\SpeedupMax{}$\times$), measures end-to-end transaction protection (\EEAMedian{}~ms classical, \EECMedian{}~ms compiled ML-KEM-768 + ML-DSA-65), and cross-validates BB84 with a protocol-level and a Qiskit circuit-level simulator (maximum mean-QBER difference \BbMaxDiff{}). Selected circuits were executed on IBM Quantum hardware (\HwJobs{} independent jobs on \HwBackend{}), showing calibration-dependent error and a clearly distinguishable intercept--resend model. The study claims no quantum advantage, no physical QKD and no practical attack on deployed cryptography.
